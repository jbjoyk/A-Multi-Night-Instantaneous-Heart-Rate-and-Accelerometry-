# Capacity Scaling, Negative Architectural Ablation, and Zero-Shot Clinical PSG Generalization of Lightweight Consumer-Smartwatch Sleep Staging: A Pre-Registered, Leakage-Free Study

**Target Venue:** *IEEE Journal of Biomedical and Health Informatics* (*IEEE J-BHI*)  
**Manuscript Version:** v2.0 (Verification-Gate Patched)

## Abstract

**Objective:** Consumer smartwatches estimate sleep stages from ~0.2 Hz instantaneous heart rate (IHR) and tri-axial accelerometry, yet clinical translation is hindered by unquantified capacity–edge trade-offs, architectural complexity assumptions, benchmark leakage, personalization tail risk, and unknown generalization to gold-standard in-lab polysomnography (PSG). We present a pre-registered, leakage-free capacity-scaling, architectural-ablation, exploratory multi-night personalization, and zero-shot external PSG validation study.

**Methods:** Under subject-disjoint 5-fold cross-validation in PhysioNet BIDSleep v1.0.1 ($N=47$ healthy adults, 246 usable nights; Apple Watch and Dreem 2 EEG), we evaluated uniform width scaling of a plain 1D CNN-BiGRU across 9 widths (`W050`–`W250`, 43,108 to 993,380 parameters), a recipe-consistent 163,940-parameter control (`W100`), and a 6-point capacity–edge Pareto grid (`p1_100k`–`p6_1051k`) against the 5,465,768-parameter official SLAMSS-IFS server architecture and frozen `Model A-164k` (163,940 parameters). We tested a pre-registered architectural hypothesis (`H-ARCH`) comparing FiLM + 2-scale multi-resolution temporal pooling (`full_v5`, 190,788 parameters) against parameter-matched plain `P190` (190,940 parameters), and enforced a pre-registered `< 400 KiB` Dynamic INT8 edge memory budget. Across $N=46$ eligible participants (152 evaluation nights), we conducted exploratory personalization analyses on the frozen `Model A` backbone (older training recipe; pending re-run on the recipe-consistent `W100` backbone), including 2-night supervised fine-tuning (`FT-2`), donor/from-scratch/unsupervised controls, 1-to-4-night scaling ($N=33$), and stage-coverage gating (`GATE_tau`). Zero-shot external validation used single-night in-lab PSG from PhysioNet SleepAccel v1.0.0 ($N=31$; $n=25$ quality-clean primary).

**Results:** Uniform width scaling with frozen `Model A` yielded a monotonic scaling trend (`H-SCALE`: Page's $L=10938.0$, $z=+2.493$, raw two-sided $p=0.0127$, Holm-adjusted two-sided $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.0254$), but retraining `Model A` under the unified recipe (`W100`: `66.68% ± 7.11%` Macro-F1; `+1.89 pp` vs. frozen `Model A` `64.79% ± 7.45%`, 95% CI `[+1.23, +2.56] pp`, raw $p=2.12\times 10^{-6}$) reduced the sensitivity trend (`H-SCALE-R`: Page's $L=10614.0$, $z=+0.265$, raw two-sided $p=0.7912$), identifying a recipe effect with saturation above ~100k parameters. Under the `< 400 KiB` Dynamic INT8 budget, compact plain backbones `p1_100k` (98,940 params, `66.67% ± 7.57%` Macro-F1, `182.00 KiB` INT8, `41.02 ms` interleaved 4-thread INT8), `W100` (163,940 params, `66.68% ± 7.11%`, `309.54 KiB` INT8, `43.38 ms` 4T INT8), and `P190` (190,940 params, `66.60% ± 6.85%` Macro-F1, `375.16 KiB` INT8, `46.46 ms` 4T INT8) narrowed 70–73% of the in-cohort deficit against 5.47M-parameter SLAMSS-IFS (`67.37% ± 6.73%`, `3186.52 ms` 4T INT8): `W100` versus `SLAMSS-IFS` differed by `-0.69 pp` (95% CI `[-1.44, +0.09] pp`, raw Wilcoxon $p=0.04777$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.1433$; the raw $p$ is below 0.05 but the Holm-adjusted $p$ is not, so a difference cannot be excluded), while `p1_100k` (`55.24×` compression) and `P190` (`28.63×`) differed by `-0.69 pp` ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.1498$) and `-0.77 pp` (raw $p=0.0727$). Conversely, `H-ARCH` failed its `+0.75 pp` practical threshold (`full_v5` `66.74% ± 6.81%` vs. `P190` `66.60% ± 6.85%`: `+0.14 pp`, 95% CI `[-0.34, +0.61] pp`, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.4037$), yielding a negative architectural ablation. On strictly zero-shot SleepAccel PSG ($n=25$, `em_off`), `P190` achieved `61.52%` single-model (`+1.01 pp` vs. `Model A`, `+0.44 pp` vs. SLAMSS-IFS, raw $p=0.5424$) and `63.42%` 15-model ensemble (`+0.48 pp` vs. `Model A`, `+0.64 pp` vs. SLAMSS-IFS, raw $p=0.7112$), while unsupervised EM prior correction (`em_on`) degraded Macro-F1 (`63.51% -> 61.30%`). Exploratory personalization on frozen `Model A` (pending `W100` re-run) yielded `+1.01 pp` for `FT-2` (inconclusive vs. `+1.50 pp`) and a positive $k=1\text{–}4$ trend (Page's $L=863.0$, $z=2.261$, raw one-sided $p=0.01187$); on higher-capacity `P190` (`ZS` `66.42%`) and `p1_100k` (`ZS` `66.84%`), exploratory `FT-2` yielded `-0.33 pp` and `-0.57 pp` while nested `GATE_tau` yielded `66.55%` and `66.74%`.

**Conclusion:** Simple plain CNN-BiGRU backbones under 400 KiB Dynamic INT8 narrow most of the in-cohort gap against 5.47M-parameter server models and generalize competitively zero-shot to clinical PSG without architectural complexity (`full_v5` negative ablation). All multi-night personalization findings on frozen `Model A` remain exploratory pending re-evaluation on the recipe-consistent `W100` backbone.

**Keywords:** Wearable sleep staging, instantaneous heart rate, capacity scaling, dynamic INT8 quantization, negative ablation, zero-shot polysomnography, multi-night personalization.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Clinical and Wearable Context
Sleep architecture is scored clinically under American Academy of Sleep Medicine (AASM) guidelines [10] into five AASM stages (W, N1, N2, N3, R), grouped into four classes (Wake, Light = N1+N2, Deep = N3, REM). While laboratory polysomnography (PSG) remains the diagnostic gold standard, its cost, obtrusive sensor montage, technologist scoring burden, and single-night susceptibility to the first-night effect preclude longitudinal multi-night monitoring in naturalistic home environments [1,3].

Consumer smartwatches equipped with optical photoplethysmography (PPG) and tri-axial accelerometers offer scalable longitudinal tracking by inferring sleep states from peripheral autonomic nervous system (ANS) tone and somatic immobility [1,3,5,8]. However, mapping low-rate instantaneous heart rate (IHR; ~0.2 Hz via Apple HealthKit) and wrist movement to central EEG-defined sleep stages is an ill-posed inverse problem subject to pronounced inter-individual biological heterogeneity in resting heart rate, autonomic responsivity, and circadian phase [1,5].

### 1.2 Capacity Scaling, Architectural Complexity, and Personalization Risk
Deploying recurrent neural architectures on consumer wearables requires balancing classification accuracy against strict memory, compute, and latency budgets on smartwatch system-on-chip (SoC) or companion smartphone hardware. A recurring question in biomedical deep learning is whether complex architectural modules (such as Feature-wise Linear Modulation [FiLM] and 2-scale multi-resolution temporal pooling) genuinely outperform simple width scaling when total parameter count is strictly matched. Answering this requires pre-registered, parameter-matched ablations rather than confounded comparisons across different capacities.

In parallel, model personalization—fine-tuning a population backbone on a few initial home nights paired with a dry-sensor EEG headband—offers a route to bridge inter-individual domain shift [5]. Yet unconstrained gradient adaptation on small, class-imbalanced personal calibration sets ($k=1\text{–}4$ nights) risks catastrophic forgetting and negative transfer, wherein fine-tuning degrades subsequent staging accuracy below the unmodified zero-shot population model [5].

### 1.3 Addressed Research Gaps and Claim Boundaries
This study addresses four foundational gaps in translational wearable sleep staging:
1. **Capacity Scaling vs. Architectural Complexity (Gap G1):** Under leakage-free 5-fold cross-validation, we quantify the exact capacity–accuracy–edge Pareto frontier of plain CNN-BiGRU models from 43.1k to 1.05M parameters, evaluate a recipe-consistent 163,940-parameter control (`W100`), select `< 400 KiB` Dynamic INT8 edge backbones (`p1_100k` and `P190`), and test via parameter-matched ablation (`H-ARCH`) whether FiLM + 2-scale multi-resolution temporal pooling (`full_v5`) provides practical benefit over plain width scaling.
2. **Protocol-Matched Server Compression Trade-Offs (Gap G2):** Published deep sleep-staging benchmarks frequently suffer from subject or night split leakage and 5-hour nocturnal cropping biases [1,7]; eliminating these confounders allows exact comparison between compact `< 400 KiB` INT8 models and the 5.47M-parameter official SLAMSS-IFS server architecture [1].
3. **Exploratory Personalization and Safety Controls in Healthy Adults (Gap G3):** Extending raw-PPG personalization findings in sleep-disordered patients [5], we conduct exploratory analyses of $k=1\text{–}4$ calibration nights, specificity/unsupervised controls, and pre-adaptation stage-coverage gating (`GATE_tau`) on the frozen `Model A` backbone (older recipe; pending re-run on `W100`) as well as on higher-capacity `p1_100k` and `P190` backbones.
4. **External Single-Night Clinical PSG Generalization and Label-Prior Shift (Gap G4):** We evaluate strictly zero-shot generalization to independent gold-standard in-lab PSG [3,4], quantify clinical sleep-summary agreement via Bland–Altman analysis [6], and test whether unsupervised Expectation-Maximization (EM) label-prior correction (`em_on`) resolves cross-modality stage-proportion bias.

We enforce explicit epistemological and clinical claim boundaries throughout this work [9]. We **do not** claim architectural novelty for FiLM or 2-scale multi-resolution temporal pooling (`H-ARCH` was not supported; reported transparently as a negative architectural ablation). We **do not** claim statistical equivalence; paired differences against SLAMSS-IFS are reported with 95% CIs, noting that a small residual difference cannot be excluded. All personalization analyses on frozen `Model A` are labelled **exploratory** and pending re-evaluation on the recipe-consistent `W100` backbone; we do not claim a confirmed personalization threshold or gating conclusion. Finally, all findings are scoped to healthy adults and offline full-night inference ($L=1200$ bidirectional GRU buffering) on smartwatch SoCs or companion smartphones rather than 1-epoch causal streaming on a 512 KB SRAM microcontroller.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Study Design, Datasets, and Participant Flow
We conducted a multi-stage computational validation across two independent, publicly available human sleep datasets in accordance with the TRIPOD+AI statement [9]. Secondary computational analysis of de-identified public archives was exempt from additional local institutional review board approval; original data collection was approved by the University of Massachusetts Lowell Institutional Review Board (BIDSleep [1,2]) and the University of Michigan Institutional Review Board (SleepAccel [3,4]), with written informed consent obtained from all participants.

The internal multi-night development, scaling, and exploratory personalization cohort was **PhysioNet BIDSleep v1.0.1** ($N=47$, doi:`10.13026/rees-1092`, ODC-By v1.0; local archive v1.0.0) [1,2], comprising 47 healthy adults (`Bidslab00`–`Bidslab68`) wearing an Apple Watch (HealthKit IHR median effective sampling rate 0.197 Hz, IQR 0.073 Hz, nightly median HR 64.0 BPM, plus tri-axial accelerometry) and a 5-channel dry-sensor Dreem 2 EEG headband scored by a single trained sleep expert (five AASM stages grouped into four classes). Of 253 recorded sessions, 7 nights across 6 participants failed a pre-registered $<60\%$ synchronized sensor coverage threshold (`Bidslab08` Night 7 [19.3%], `Bidslab16` Night 2 [24.0%], `Bidslab34` Night 1 [58.7%], `Bidslab42` Nights 1 [30.1%] and 3 [1.1%], `Bidslab49` Night 1 [12.4%], and `Bidslab60` Night 1 [33.8%]), leaving 246 usable nights across 47 participants. Recomputed directly from raw labels across all 246 usable nights ($N=47$), the masked usable epoch pool comprised 203,338 valid 30-s epochs (~826.6/night: 10.83% Wake, 44.20% Light, 18.91% Deep, 26.06% REM), raw unmasked pooled labels (209,482 epochs) were 11.06% Wake, 44.60% Light, 18.54% Deep, and 25.79% REM, raw unmasked participant-averaged shares ($N=47$) were 10.96% Wake, 44.39% Light, 18.63% Deep, and 26.02% REM, and the legacy 31-subject single training split (`137,801` epochs) was 11.9% Wake, 46.2% Light, 18.6% Deep, and 23.2% REM.

Three nested analytical sub-cohorts were defined a priori within BIDSleep: (1) the population baseline and scaling cohort ($N=47$, 246 nights, subject-disjoint 5-fold outer CV); (2) the two-night adaptation cohort ($N=46$, 152 evaluation nights, requiring $n_{\text{usable}} \ge 3$ and excluding `Bidslab49` with 2 usable nights); and (3) the calibration-depth cohort ($k \in \{1,2,3,4\}$) evaluated on both available cohorts ($E_k$: $N=46$ [198 nights] at $k=1$, $N=46$ [152] at $k=2$, $N=42$ [106] at $k=3$, $N=33$ [64] at $k=4$) and the fixed common cohort ($E_4$, $N=33$, 64 nights).

The external zero-shot validation cohort was **PhysioNet SleepAccel v1.0.0** ($N=31$, doi:`10.13026/hmhs-py35`) [3,4], comprising 31 healthy adults each contributing one single-night Apple Watch + in-lab PSG recording. Pre-flight quality screening excluded 6 recordings prior to comparative inference: 5 short PSG recordings ($<600$ epochs: `3509524` [417], `4018081` [510], `46343` [567], `5132496` [465], `759667` [475]) and 1 accelerometer dropout (`7749105` [960 epochs, 13.65% valid motion coverage]), yielding $n=25$ quality-clean primary participants and $N=31$ all-subject sensitivity participants. In-lab PSG exhibited a reference stage prior of 8.24% Wake, 57.74% Light, 11.08% Deep, and 22.94% REM across 22,633 masked epochs per checkpoint (12.01% Deep and 25.21% REM of TST on $n=25$). Zero-support stage F1 (`1455390` with 0 Deep epochs in $n=25$; `5132496` and `7749105` with 0 REM epochs in $N=31$) was defined deterministically as 0.0.

### 2.2 Input Representation, Capacity-Scaling Grid, and Edge Quantization
Full-night nocturnal sequences of length $L=1200$ thirty-second epochs (10.0 hours; zero-padded if shorter, stitched via 50%-overlapping sliding-window logits if longer) were constructed from 1 Hz resampled IHR, tri-axial accelerometry magnitude, jerk, and energy features with robust per-night median/IQR normalization. Full-night context ($L=1200$) was selected over 5-hour cropping ($L=600$) [1], which discards 28% of valid epochs, depletes late-night REM, and lowered `Model A` Macro-F1 from 64.79% to 63.06%. All models received identical circadian-neutralized subject metadata (effective sampling rate, nightly HR mean/SD, with clock-time cosine/sine zeroed).

To characterize capacity scaling and edge efficiency, we evaluated two pre-registered width-scaling designs plus a recipe-consistent retrained control on top of the plain 1D CNN-BiGRU architecture (3-stage 1D Conv encoder with kernels $\{3,5,5\}$ and spatial dropout $p=0.20$, 2-layer BiGRU, linear head) under identical 5-fold $\times$ 3-seed (42, 123, 2024) training:
1. **6-Point Capacity–Edge Pareto Grid (75 runs):** `p1_100k` (98,940 params), `p2_164k` (frozen `Model A` anchor, 163,940 params), `p3_268k` (268,260 params), `p4_404k` (403,524 params), `p5_666k` (665,556 params), and `p6_1051k` (1,051,492 params). Deterministic elbow selection (zero SleepAccel look-ahead) targeted the smallest model within $\le 0.20\text{ pp}$ ($\ge 90\%$ gain) of $m_{\max}$ (`66.6726%`), selecting **`p1_100k`** (`CNN-BiGRU-Star`, 98,940 params).
2. **9-Width Single-Factor Sweep & Parameter-Matched Ablation (105 new runs + reused anchors):** Uniform width multiplier $w \in \{0.50, 0.75, 1.00, 1.08, 1.17, 1.25, 1.50, 2.00, 2.50\}$ yielding `W050` (43,108 params), `W075` (93,796), `A` (163,940), **`P190`** (190,940), `B` (223,268), `W125` (253,540), `W150` (362,596), `W200` (639,076), and `W250` (993,380). Under pre-registered `1-SE` selection constrained to `< 400 KiB` Dynamic INT8 memory (excluding `full_v5` prior to downstream evaluation), **`P190`** (parameter-matched plain control, `d_enc=72` [×1.125], `d_temp=50`/dir [×1.04], 190,940 params, `375.16 KiB` INT8, gap `0.0120 pp <= SE_boot 0.1545 pp` vs. $m^*=$ `B` `66.61%`) was selected. To test `H-ARCH` without parameter-count confounding, `P190` (190,940 params) was matched against `full_v5` (190,788 params, FiLM + 2-scale multi-resolution temporal pooling). Under Amendment A1, `W100` (exact 163,940-param `Model A` architecture) was retrained under the unified width-sweep recipe (15 runs).
3. **Server Reference (`SLAMSS-IFS`):** Official repository implementation [1] (5,465,768 trainable params including its 4.64M-param `cnn3` fusion block [4,637,696 params, 84.9% of model] and 299,496-param `s2s` [186,432 encoder + 79,016 decoder + 34,048 remaining] vs. 666,060 in the paper text), trained under identical 5-fold splits with zero teacher-forcing and FP32 composite loss.

Post-training Dynamic INT8 quantization quantized recurrent BiGRU and linear layers (~68.9–70.5% of parameters) while retaining Conv1D/normalization in FP32, profiling exact MMACs, FP32 and INT8 weight payloads, and interleaved round-robin 4-thread (4T) / 1-thread (1T) CPU latency for $L=1200$.

### 2.3 Exploratory Personalization Protocols, Controls, and Gating
All personalization evaluations on `Model A` ($N=46$) were computed on the frozen `Model A` backbone (older training recipe) and are **exploratory**, pending re-run on the recipe-consistent `W100` backbone. Five arms were evaluated on post-calibration evaluation nights (night 3 onward): frozen `ZS`, 2-night fine-tuning (`FT-2`, Adam, $B=2$, nested inner-validation selection of LR, epochs, and unfreezing depth), donor fine-tuning (`FT-other-2`, `S1`), from-scratch training (`PS-2`, `S2`), and remediated quality-gated unsupervised InfoMax (`UA-2`, `S3`, 81,233 epochs, 97.26% valid coverage). Multi-depth scaling ($k=1\text{–}4$) was reconciled via CPU-only frozen-checkpoint `ZS` evaluation (`63.54%` on $E_4$, `64.58%` on $E_2$). Exploratory analyses tested 6 baseline covariates (with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR and Oldham's headroom check [12]), KL-regularized `FT-2-KL`, and stage-coverage gating (`GATE_tau`, reverting to `ZS` when $\min_c P_{\text{cal}}(Y=c) < \tau$). For `p1_100k` and `P190`, `FT-2` and nested per-fold `GATE_tau` ($\tau^* \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$; Folds 1–2 selected $\tau^*=0.05$, Folds 3–5 selected $\tau^*=0.01$, abstaining 3/46 subjects total) were evaluated identically.

### 2.4 Statistical Analysis and Clinical Agreement
Primary metrics were participant-level unweighted 4-class Macro-F1 averaged over 3 seeds (15 checkpoints across 5 folds $\times$ 3 seeds) with sample standard deviations (`ddof=1`). Paired comparisons used 10,000-resample participant-level percentile bootstrap 95% CIs (`seed=42`), two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Cohen's $d_z$, and Holm–Bonferroni step-down corrections. `H-SCALE` (monotonic trend across 9 ordered plain widths) used Page's $L$ test [11] with Holm-adjusted two-sided $p < 0.05$. `H-ARCH` required `full_v5 - P190` to exceed `+0.75 pp` practically with $p_{\text{Holm}} < 0.05$. On SleepAccel, `em_off` (strictly zero-shot without target-prior adaptation) was evaluated under primary single-model mean and secondary 15-model probability ensemble modes, alongside Bland–Altman clinical agreement [6] (mean bias, 95% LoA, $\text{SE}(\text{LoA})$, MAE, Pearson $r$, proportional-bias slope, Shapiro–Wilk normality). Unsupervised EM label-prior correction (`em_on`) was tested as a negative prior-shift check.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Capacity–Accuracy–Edge Pareto Scaling ($N=47$, `ddof=1`)
Across the 9 ordered plain CNN-BiGRU widths (**Table 1**, **Figure 1A**), Macro-F1 increased from `W050` (43,108 params, `65.61% ± 7.48%`) to a broad plateau across `p1_100k` (98,940 params, `66.67% ± 7.57%`), `W100` (163,940 params, `66.68% ± 7.11%`), `P190` (190,940 params, `66.60% ± 6.85%`), `B` (223,268 params, `66.61% ± 7.06%`), and `W125` (253,540 params, `66.70% ± 6.80%`), before saturating at `W150`–`W250` (`66.01%`–`66.34%`). With frozen `Model A`, `H-SCALE` yielded Page's $L = 10938.0$ ($\mu_L = 10575.0$, $\sigma_L = 145.43$, $z = +2.493$, raw one-sided $p = 0.00634$, raw two-sided $p = 0.01268$, Holm-adjusted two-sided $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.0254 < 0.05$).

Under the pre-registered `< 400 KiB` Dynamic INT8 budget, all three compact plain backbones outperformed frozen `Model A-164k` (`64.79% ± 7.45%`) and narrowed 70–73% of the in-cohort deficit against 5,465,768-parameter `SLAMSS-IFS` (`67.37% ± 6.73%`; a small residual difference cannot be excluded):
- **`p1_100k` (`CNN-BiGRU-Star`):** Achieved `66.67% ± 7.57%` Macro-F1 (`+1.88 pp` vs. frozen `Model A-164k`, 95% CI `[+1.25, +2.49]`, raw $p=5.95\times 10^{-7}$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=1.19\times 10^{-6}$, $d_z=+0.875$, `40/47` wins; REM F1 `+3.56 pp`, raw $p=2.40\times 10^{-10}$). Against `SLAMSS-IFS`, `p1_100k` differed by `-0.69 pp` (95% CI `[-1.57, +0.15]`, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.1498$, $d_z=-0.229$, `17/47` wins) at `55.24×` parameter compression. Dynamic INT8 required `182.00 KiB` in-memory (`207.97 KiB` serialized; `52.9%` reduction from `386.48 KiB` FP32), `456.1 MMACs`, and `41.02 ms` interleaved 4T (`47.07 ms` 1T) INT8 latency with paired INT8–FP32 difference `-0.0134 pp` (95% CI `[-0.0489, +0.0210]`, raw $p=0.4656$).
- **`W100` (Amendment A1 Retrained Control, `R1: recipe effect`):** Retraining the exact `Model A` architecture (`163,940` params; 62/62 state-dict keys and shapes verified) under the unified width-sweep recipe yielded **`66.68% ± 7.11%`** Macro-F1, improving over frozen `Model A` (`64.79% ± 7.45%`) by **`+1.89 pp`** (95% CI `[+1.23, +2.56]`, raw $p=2.12\times 10^{-6}$, $d_z=+0.811$) and matching `P190` (`66.60% ± 6.85%`; `Δ_P = +0.08 pp`, 95% CI `[-0.29, +0.46]`, $p_{\text{Holm}}=1.0000$). Against `SLAMSS-IFS`, **`W100` differed by `-0.69 pp` (95% CI `[-1.44, +0.09]`, raw Wilcoxon $p=0.04777$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.1433$)**—the raw $p$ is below 0.05 but the Holm-adjusted $p$ is not, so a difference cannot be excluded. Replacing frozen `Model A` with `W100` in sensitivity `H-SCALE-R` yielded Page's $L=10614.0$ ($z=+0.265$, raw two-sided $p=0.7912$), confirming that the apparent 164k–191k jump was a training-recipe effect (`R1: recipe effect`) and that plain CNN-BiGRU capacity saturates within the `~0.4–0.5 pp` noise floor above ~100k params.
- **`P190` (`1-SE` Selection):** Achieved `66.60% ± 6.85%` Macro-F1 (`+1.80 pp` vs. frozen `Model A-164k`, 95% CI `[+1.14, +2.50]`, raw $p=2.96\times 10^{-6}$), differing from `SLAMSS-IFS` by `-0.77 pp` (95% CI `[-1.59, +0.07]`, raw $p=0.0727$) at `28.63×` compression. Dynamic INT8 required `375.16 KiB` in-memory (`402.91 KiB` serialized; `49.7%` reduction from `745.86 KiB` FP32), `984.2 MMACs`, and `46.46 ms` interleaved 4T (`59.41 ms` 1T) INT8 latency with paired INT8–FP32 difference `+0.0244 pp` (95% CI `[+0.0017, +0.0480]`, raw $p=0.0836$).
- **Interleaved Round-Robin CPU Latency (`AMD Ryzen 7 7700 8-Core Processor`, `L=1200`, `batch=1`, 30 warm-up + 200 timed passes):** All Dynamic INT8 runs satisfied `CV <= 5%`: 4T / 1T INT8 medians were `p1_100k` `41.02 / 47.07 ms`, `W100` `43.38 / 53.63 ms`, `full_v5` `31.53 / 43.73 ms`, `P190` `46.46 / 59.41 ms`, and `Official SLAMSS-IFS` `3186.52 / 3292.75 ms`.

### 3.2 Negative Architectural Ablation (`H-ARCH`)
Comparing `full_v5` (FiLM + 2-scale multi-resolution temporal pooling, 190,788 parameters, `66.74% ± 6.81%` Macro-F1) directly against parameter-matched plain `P190` (190,940 parameters, `66.60% ± 6.85%`) across $N=47$ participants (**Figure 1B**) yielded $\bar{\Delta} = +0.14\text{ pp}$ (`+0.1442 pp`, 95% bootstrap CI `[-0.34, +0.61] pp`, raw Wilcoxon $p = 0.4037$, $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.4037$, $d_z = +0.086$, `26W/21L`). Because the 95% CI upper bound (`+0.61 pp`) fell below the pre-registered `+0.75 pp` practical threshold and $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.4037$, `H-ARCH` was **`NOT SUPPORTED (practically)`**, and we report this transparently as a negative architectural ablation with zero architectural novelty claimed.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 Capacity Scaling, Negative Architectural Ablation, and Edge Deployment
Our pre-registered capacity-scaling and recipe-control experiments overturn two common assumptions in wearable sleep staging. First, plain CNN-BiGRU models under `< 400 KiB` Dynamic INT8 (`p1_100k`: `182.00 KiB` INT8, `41.02 ms` 4T INT8; `W100`: `309.54 KiB` INT8, `43.38 ms` 4T INT8; `P190`: `375.16 KiB` INT8, `46.46 ms` 4T INT8) capture 70–73% of the in-cohort gap against 5.47M-parameter `SLAMSS-IFS` (`W100` vs. `SLAMSS-IFS`: `-0.69 pp`, 95% CI `[-1.44, +0.09]`, raw Wilcoxon $p=0.04777$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.1433$; a difference cannot be excluded) and show no statistically significant difference on zero-shot external in-lab PSG (`P190` `61.52%` / `63.42%` vs. `SLAMSS-IFS` `61.08%` / `62.79%`). Second, parameter-matched ablation (`H-ARCH`) demonstrates that FiLM and 2-scale multi-resolution temporal pooling (`full_v5`) add only `+0.14 pp` ($p_{\text{Holm}}=0.4037$), failing practical significance. Reporting this negative ablation transparently prevents unwarranted architectural complexity in resource-constrained wearable pipelines.

### 4.2 External PSG Label-Prior Shift and Exploratory Personalization Boundaries
Both compact and server models systematically overestimate Deep% and REM% on SleepAccel PSG relative to Dreem 2 home EEG training labels. Crucially, the `em_on` negative check (`63.51% -> 61.30%`) is consistent with deeper scoring/covariate differences between dry-sensor Dreem 2 EEG and laboratory PSG rather than simple marginal prior shift. All personalization analyses on frozen `Model A` (including $k=1\text{–}4$ scaling and `GATE_tau`) remain strictly exploratory pending re-evaluation on the recipe-consistent `W100` backbone; on `p1_100k` and `P190`, exploratory 2-night `FT-2` yielded no aggregate gain.

### 4.3 Limitations and Future Prospective Targets
All conclusions are scoped to healthy adults; prospective validation in clinical sleep-disorder cohorts (e.g., PhysioNet DREAMT v2.2.0) and multi-night external cohorts (e.g., Simons Sleep Project, SFARI Base DS0000089) is required before diagnostic deployment. SleepAccel provides only one PSG night per participant, precluding external multi-night personalization testing. Finally, full-night $L=1200$ bidirectional GRU inference requires offline buffering on smartwatch SoCs or companion smartphones rather than 1-epoch causal streaming on bare-metal 512 KB SRAM microcontrollers.
